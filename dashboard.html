    How it works

    Plain rules, tested on past data, run with paper money.

    How it picks, buys and sells

    1. Scan: every weekday morning it pulls the full list of ~7,000 US stocks and keeps ~2,100 real companies.
    2. Predict: it scores each by how fast and how steadily it climbed over 90 trading days. Steady climbers tend to keep going.
    3. Invest: it buys the 7 strongest, putting more money in the strongest trends (never over 35% in one stock), but only while the S&P 500 is above its 200-day average.
    4. Wait: when the overall market is falling it buys nothing new and keeps cash.
    5. Sell and move on: the morning after a holding closes below its 100-day average it sells, and won't buy that stock again for 90 days. Every 4 weeks it also swaps out anything that dropped out of the top 10.
    6. Watch: it checks every holding every 30 seconds during market hours and trades at real prices. Safety net: if a holding falls 25% under its best close since buying, it's sold right away and banned for 90 days.
    7. Rescan: every 30 seconds during market hours it also re-ranks the whole market on live prices (including 440+ foreign companies listed in the US) and flags holdings near their sell line or safety net. The Live portfolio tab shows the last rescan and what it changed.
    8. Read the news: it checks recent headlines for every holding and every planned buy. Serious bad news is a veto: it won't buy that stock, and sells it if it owns it. Milder warning signs are marked caution.
    9. Risk level: the Invested figure shows how much of the account is in stocks. 100% means fully invested; above 100% means it is using borrowed (paper) money, which makes both gains and losses bigger.
    10. Adapt and learn: its picks and bet sizes change with the market every rebalance, it stops borrowing when the market turns down, and stocks that burned it are banned for 90 days. It reviews its own closed trades, and once a month it replays the past year with 27 other settings as a report. It doesn't switch to last year's winner, because that lost money in testing.

    Connecting a real account (optional)

    1. How money gets in: a bank can't plug into a bot directly. You open a brokerage account at Alpaca (alpaca.markets, built for trading bots, no commissions) and move money from your bank into it inside Alpaca's app. The bot never sees bank details.
    2. Start with Alpaca's paper account: it's free and gives fake money. In Alpaca, open Paper Trading, create API keys, then in GitHub go to the repo's Settings, Secrets and variables, Actions, and add two secrets: ALPACA_KEY_ID and ALPACA_SECRET_KEY. From the next check, the bot copies this portfolio into that account in the same proportions, and the Brokerage link card shows it.
    3. Real money, later: needs an account holder who is 18 or older. Use the live keys and add a repository variable ALPACA_MODE = live. The copy never borrows unless you set ALPACA_MAX_EXPOSURE above 1. Run it on paper for a few weeks first, and only put in money you can afford to lose: past tests are not a promise.
