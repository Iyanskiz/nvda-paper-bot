Honest caveat: these tests use today's list of stocks, which leaves out companies that went bankrupt or shrank, so every result looks better than reality. The equal-weight row holds every stock on the same list and has the same bias: the fair test is beating that row, not just the S&P 500.

How it picks, buys and sells

  1. Scan: every weekday morning it pulls the full list of ~7,000 US stocks and keeps ~2,100 real companies.
  2. Predict: it scores each by how fast and how steadily it climbed over 90 trading days. Steady climbers tend to keep going.
  3. Invest: it buys the 7 strongest in equal amounts, but only while the S&P 500 is above its 200-day average.
  4. Wait: when the overall market is falling it buys nothing new and keeps cash.
  5. Sell and move on: the morning after a holding closes below its 100-day average it sells, and won't buy that stock again for 90 days. Every 4 weeks it also swaps out anything that dropped out of the top 14.
  6. Watch: it checks every holding every 5 minutes during market hours and trades at real prices.
